# KWS onset estimators (rightkit-asr)

Owner: the HeardRight KWS lane (Adrian assigned the KWS objective; Fab2 disclaimed rightkit-asr; the Codex chat is advisory only). Issue: rightkit/rightkit#1273. Status: source only until the owner-approved compile, test and replay run.

## D4 fricative onset estimator

Default off, diagnostic. `OnsetConfig.fricative` (`FricativeParams`: window 250 ms, floor 500 ms, rise 1.0, minimum run 40 ms, vowel window 60 ms, tilt margin 1.0, every value settable) adds `KeywordOnset.fricative`; it never replaces `emission`, `aligned`, `posterior` or `acoustic`. A hiss run counts only when a vowel entry starts within one frame after it and inside the vowel window before the anchor (a temporal-association heuristic, not proof of the same occurrence); a run shorter than the minimum is dropped before ambiguity is counted. It abstains with a named reason (`invalid_evidence`, `floor_short`, `before_epoch`, `no_transition`, `run_too_short`, `ambiguous_transitions`, `window_limit`) and never fabricates a boundary. `KwsSwitches.fricative_strict` (default off) returns no boundary when D4 has no candidate instead of falling back to emission. Qualification is by native replay on the frozen scorer against the matched baseline in the same binary; the owner corpus is evaluation only.

## Firing trace (default off)

Owner: the HeardRight KWS lane (issue rightkit#1533). Status: source only, uncompiled and untested until the owner-approved compile and replay. Purpose: observe, in the real build, the beam facts the offline port could not recover (the firing's decoder context and path, which hypotheses were alive, what was pruned or expired, and why a confirm stream did not fire). It is pure observation: nothing it records is read back by the decoder, so beam order, pruning, `ys_probs`, timestamps and firing cannot depend on it.

Switch: `KwsSwitches.trace: Option<KwsTraceConfig>` (`None` is the default); `rightkit_asr::kws::KeywordSpotterConfig.trace: Option<TraceConfig>` underneath. With it off a stream holds no trace state: the decoder pays one `Option` check per chunk and one `bool` check per step, allocates nothing, and every existing report field and replay row is unchanged. The only text change with it off is the load log line, whose `switches=` Debug gains `trace: None`.

Records, each bounded in count with a dropped counter instead of silent omission, and bounded in bytes: every path history (`ys`, `timestamps`, `ys_probs`) in a firing, hypothesis, top, or merge record keeps only its last `history_tokens` entries (default 16, `--trace-history`), with the original lengths and a `truncated` flag beside it.
- `FiringTrace` (wake and confirm streams; `firing: true`): crate version, keyword and config hashes (FNV-1a 64, with the byte lengths of the three model files; not a model content hash), occurrence counter, keyword; the clocks (`TraceClock`: reset epoch, the feature-frame origin of the current epoch on the clock of `KeywordResult::frames`, feature frames ready, the encoder-consumed extent, samples handed to the stream; these are input counters, never recognition wall time); and the firing at the exact pre-reset event, snapshotted at the firing step before a later hit in the same chunk can overwrite the result: `Firing.context`, the selected path's `ys`, `timestamps`, `ys_probs`, `fired_frame`, `num_trailing_blanks`, score, threshold, and the selected plus up to three other hypotheses alive. At most 16 are held between drains.
- `StepTrace` (confirm stream; `window_frames` above 0): one record per decoded encoder frame from `window_frames` before the wake candidate's first token to `window_frames` after its end (on the confirm stream's own frame clock), up to `capacity_steps` between drains (default 512; later steps are counted in `steps_dropped`). Per step: hypotheses scored and hypotheses after the merge, whether TTL expiry emptied the beam, the top hypothesis (keyword-prefix progress, first-token frame, trailing blanks, token probabilities), the keyword check on it when its state completes a keyword (mean token score against the threshold, and the trailing-blank condition), whether it fired, the paths the active-path TTL dropped (with each one's first-token frame), the keyword paths pruned (none of their children entered the top-k beam), the merges on keyword paths (the timestamps and probabilities each side held, and which side survived), and the keyword-extension candidates: for each parent hypothesis (keyword parents first, then the root, whose extensions are first-token candidates) the tokens that advance it along a keyword, each with its token probability, its selection score, its rank (candidates with a strictly greater score this step; ties unbroken), whether it entered the top-k beam, the beam's cutoff (the lowest selected score), and the parent's best other selected child (token and score). At most eight extensions are recorded per step and the rest are counted in `extensions_omitted`; the whole vocabulary is never logged. Extensions are the parent's direct graph arcs; fail transitions are not followed, and the scores are the selection scores before the keyword boost, so a boost that would have reordered children is not visible. `pruned` still means only that no child of that parent entered the beam; the extension records are what explain an extension that lost while another child survived. The per-step event lists are bounded by the beam width.
- `KwsFeedEvent` (cascade): create, suffix, duplicate, stale, gap, destroy and expire events with the snapshot start, live total, stream origin, samples fed and accepted, pending samples, the cascade reset epoch, and the wake candidate's id, extent and expiry deadline (end plus the confirm timeout).

Surfacing: `Kws::take_trace()` drains `KwsTrace` (stream records collected before any stream was destroyed, plus the feed events); each firing also logs `kws_firing_trace`. `kws_onset_replay --trace`, `--trace-window FRAMES` and `--trace-capacity STEPS` add a `trace` key to each JSONL row (a `TRACE` line in text mode); with none of them the output is unchanged.

Streams: wake and confirm streams are drained on every `take_trace` and before destruction, reset or expiry (a candidate expiring or a gap destroys the confirm stream; its records are kept first). Fresh-confirmation decodes (stage `fresh_confirm`, with the wake candidate's id, collected after the whole decode so a no-fire is recorded) and the idle-wake stream (stage `idle_wake`, collected when it stops or on `take_trace`) are collected too. The replay-only full-command decode and the hey-wake window check create their streams without trace state, so nothing allocates where it cannot be read. The no-fire step window rides the confirm stream. A fresh-confirm stream gets the same window semantics before its first sample is fed: encoder frames around the wake candidate's region on the stream's own clock (the stream starts at the snapshot start; stream `window` is `candidate`). When the snapshot's absolute origin is unknown the stream instead keeps its newest `capacity_steps` steps (older ones are dropped and counted) and its `window` is `unknown_origin`. Provenance: the fresh decode declares its synthetic 200 ms silence tail as padding, so every record's `TraceClock` carries `padding_samples_fed` apart from `samples_fed` (which includes it; real audio fed is the difference, and `samples_fed` is never real-audio latency on a stream given padding), and the fresh stream record carries `real_samples_fed` and `tail_samples_fed` as actually fed before the decode stopped. The feed schedule and stop-on-first-result are unchanged.

Limits: padding provenance is the caller's (the crate cannot tell real from synthetic samples; the trace counts samples fed and nothing about their origin). A history cut to its last entries can drop a keyword's first token from the retained list; `first_token_frame` fields are computed from the full path before the cut. A merge replay under another `merge_timestamps` policy is a counterfactual that needs its own run, not this log. `Hypotheses::add` is unit-tested for identical results with and without the observer (both merge policies, ties, both math paths); a trace-on versus trace-off replay comparison is still owed and belongs after the owner-approved compile. Qualification still needs recognition parity (detections, keywords, frames and probabilities bit-identical on and off on the owner clips and the 373-clip roster) and an overhead run that varies only trace collection.

## Stale-token gap guard (default off)

Owner: the HeardRight KWS lane (plan arms B28 to B30, `docs/plans/2026-11-07-kws-lever-matrix.md`). Status: source only, uncompiled and untested until the owner-approved compile and replay. The guard changes what can be detected, so it needs the same qualification as any decoder lever; only the offline-port result (`2026-11-09-kws-beam-port-gap-guard.md`, a port hypothesis, not a live-path claim) exists for it.

Switch: `KwsSwitches.gap_guard: Option<i32>` (`None` is the default, today's behaviour), `KeywordSpotterConfig.stale_gap_frames` underneath, replay flag `--gap-guard G`. G is in encoder frames (40 ms each), validated 1 to 1000; the plan's arms are 8, 12 and 16. It is set on the wake and confirm spotters and not on the hey-wake spotter (the port runs covered wake and confirm). With it off the decoder takes one `Option` check per emitted keyword token, allocates nothing, and every existing field and replay row is unchanged.

Semantics, matching the offline port (`beam_port.py`, `gap_guard=G`):
- Checked when a non-blank, non-unk token is emitted and the path's new graph state is still on the keyword graph (a path that leaves it resets to the blank context and is not checked). With the new state's depth `L` at least 2 and at least `L` timestamps on the path, the newest consecutive pair is tested: drop when `ts[-1] - ts[-2] > G`. A gap of exactly G is kept; G + 1 is dropped. Earlier pairs were tested when they were emitted; a first token (depth 1) has no pair, so is never dropped.
- Applied where the port applies it: before the candidate is inserted into the new beam, so a dropped path takes part in no merge and no log-add, and nothing refills the beam; it can hold fewer than `max_active_paths` hypotheses. When every candidate is dropped the beam becomes the single blank hypothesis. Top-k selection, the keyword boost, TTL expiry (which runs at the start of the next step, from the path's first token), merge policy, thresholds, trailing blanks and the firing reset are unchanged. The port's firing-step re-check of the whole matched chain is a safety log there, expected never to trigger, and is not reproduced.
- Not in the port: the guard is not applied to the hey-wake spotter.

Evidence: `KeywordDetection`, `Firing` and `KwsOnsetReport` carry `gap_drops` (paths dropped since the stream's last reset, up to the firing) and `gap_drops_chunk` (those in the firing's chunk); both are `0` with the guard off. The replay adds a `gap_guard` key to each JSONL row (a `GAP` line in text mode) with the setting and these counts per cascade report; with the guard off the key is absent. A clip that never fires has no report, so its drops appear only in the trace: with the trace on, each drop is a `TraceGapDrop` in the stream records (frame, gap, depth, the active tokens and frames, the offending pair, and the path's score before the emission), bounded to 64 between drains with a dropped counter. The existing log lines are unchanged.

Gap observation (guard on or off): each firing also records `max_gap_frames`, the largest gap between consecutive emitted keyword tokens (every pair, as the port's firing re-check does; `0` for one token), and `gap_violation`, true only with the guard on and `max_gap_frames` above G (expected never). It is read off the emitted chain after the fact and never filters or changes anything. Both are on `KeywordDetection` and `KwsOnsetReport`; the replay reports them under `--gap-guard` or `--report-gap` (a `gap_guard` row key and a closing `gap_summary` that counts reports and violations separately from the drop counts).

Reading rule for the trace: a `selected` extension in a step record means selected by the PRE-guard top-k, not that the resulting path survived the guard; a guard drop appears separately as a `TraceGapDrop`.

## Encoder state buffer reuse (default off)

Plan arm C7, a speed candidate. Status: source only; identical outputs and any speedup are UNVERIFIED until a replay with the same binary compares detections, timestamps, scores and timings with the trace off. Switch: `KwsSwitches.encoder_state_reuse` (`KeywordSpotterConfig.encoder_state_reuse`), replay flag `--encoder-state-reuse`.

What is reused: each encoder call returns the next cached states, which `run_encoder` copies into freshly allocated tensors. With the switch on, `run_encoder_reuse` copies the same outputs into the buffers the previous call left in the model (one spare set per model, shared by its spotters, a few MB), then swaps them in as the stream's states and keeps the old ones as the next spare. Identity argument: the values written are exactly the encoder's output bytes, the same copy the plain path makes; a reused buffer is cleared before it is refilled, so no earlier value can remain; shapes are copied the same way; states, inputs, features and the order of calls are untouched, so the encoder sees identical inputs and the decoder identical outputs. The only difference is where the bytes live. On an error nothing is committed, as before. The cost is the spare set of buffers. Validation must cover INTERLEAVED wake and confirm streams on one shared model, including a reset in the middle, not a single stream only: the spare set is per model, so the test is that a state buffer never carries a value across streams or across a reset.

## Finite keyword-context decoder table (default off)

Plan arm C8, a speed candidate. Status: source only; identical outputs and any speedup are UNVERIFIED until the same replay comparison. Switch: `KwsSwitches.decoder_table` (`KeywordSpotterConfig.decoder_table`), replay flag `--decoder-table`.

What is precomputed: the decoder's output depends only on its `context_size` input tokens. A path on the keyword graph is the blank context `[-1, 0]` followed by keyword tokens (it resets to blank on leaving the graph), so every context the decoder can see is a window of that sequence: the blank context, `[0, t]` for each keyword token `t`, and every pair of keyword tokens. At spotter creation the table of those contexts (tokens read from the keyword graph; context size from the model; a few hundred rows, capped at 4096 rows, beyond which no table is built and the plain path stays) is run through the decoder in one batch and kept in the model, keyed by context alone, so spotters sharing a model may add rows without conflict. At decode, a context in the table is served from it; any other context runs the decoder as one batch in first-seen order, as without the switch. It takes precedence over `decoder_cache` when both are on. A DECLINED table is off for that spotter and falls back to what is configured: `decoder_cache` if set, else the plain decoder (`options.decoder_table` is cleared, so nothing deduplicates against another graph's rows). Declined means this graph alone needs more than 4096 rows (`graph_over_cap`), or the model's table already holds rows such that existing plus this graph's new rows would pass 4096 (`model_table_full`); admission is all or nothing and checks the AGGREGATE count of the shared model before inserting, so the cap bounds the model's table, not each graph's. An admitted spotter may be served rows another graph put in the model's table: they are the decoder's rows for the same context, so valid under the same precondition below. `DecoderTableReport` (`KeywordSpotter::decoder_table_report`, `Kws::decoder_table_report`, a `kws_decoder_mode` log line when the switch is on, a `decoder_table` replay key and `DECODER_TABLE` text line) gives the EFFECTIVE mode (`table`, `cache` or `plain`), the decline reason, the startup precompute time in ms, this graph's rows, and the model's retained rows and bytes (row values plus context keys; the map's own overhead is not counted).

Prewarm batch: the table is built in ONE decoder call whose batch is every new row (about 1 + K + K squared for K keyword tokens: for the shipped wake and confirm lists a few hundred rows, up to 4096), whereas per-step decoding uses batches of 1 to `max_active_paths` rows. Batch invariance must therefore be established at those actual sizes (the table batch size of each spotter and 1 to 8), on the same provider and platform the replay runs on, not only at the sizes `kws_decoder_is_batch_invariant` covers today. The replay must report the startup precompute time, the table rows and the retained table bytes beside steady decode timing, all with the trace off (so trace collection does not pollute the timing).

Identity argument and its precondition: a table row is the decoder's output for that context, computed in a different batch (the table batch) than the per-step batches of the plain path. Rows are identical only where the decoder's rows do not depend on the batch they are computed in, the same precondition as `decoder_cache`, established for the fp32 decoder on x86-64 by `kws_decoder_is_batch_invariant` and not established elsewhere. The int8 joiner is not table-served and still scores the whole beam together. So where that precondition holds, every score, timestamp and detection is bit-identical; where it does not, they can differ, which is why the replay comparison (and the batch-invariance test on the host) decides. Left out: no lazy table growth and no table for graphs over 4096 rows.

## Shared front end C6 (default off)

Plan arm C6, DETECTION-CHANGING. Status: source only, uncompiled and untested; the frozen design is `heardright/docs/architecture/2026-11-09-kws-c6-shared-frontend-design.md`. Switch: `KwsSwitches.shared_front_end` (`KeywordSpotterConfig.shared_front_end: Option<FrontEndConfig>` on the wake spotter), replay flag `--shared-front-end`. Thresholds are unchanged and are not tuned on the owner roster; no CPU saving is claimed before isolated encoder-versus-total CPU measurements exist.

What is shared: the wake stream's encoder run. After each chunk the wake stream appends its encoder frames to a ring on the WAKE clock (an integer encoder-frame clock, 640 samples a frame) and, when onset estimation is on, to a second onset history for the confirm beam. The LIVE confirm beam (`BeamStream`, no filterbank, no encoder state, no audio) decodes retained frames on the confirm spotter's own decoder and joiner. Fresh confirmation is untouched: its own stream, its 200 ms zero tail and its own encoder; its outcome is reported apart from the live one.

Clock and start: `requested_frame = floor((candidate.start_sample - 32,000 - wake_origin_sample) / 640)` in signed integers, rounded by floor toward minus infinity (frozen before any result), so the beam never starts later than the request. A negative request is clamped to the stream origin (`origin_clamped`); a request older than the ring's oldest frame moves to it (`ring_truncated`). They are separate flags; `state` is `full`, `origin_clamped`, `ring_truncated` or both, and only `full` is the requested context. The beam's frames, `fired_frame` and the report `origin_sample` are on the wake clock.

Retention (a proposal from the constants in the code, in 40 ms frames): requested context 50, candidate span at most 50 (`MAX_KEYWORD_SPAN_SECONDS`), confirm timeout 38 (`CONFIRM_TIMEOUT_SAMPLES` rounded up), detection latency 16 (one chunk of 8, one trailing blank, a probe allowance): 154, ring 160 frames (6.4 s). Confirm onset history: encoder frames `ring + align_lead 10 + posterior_max 15` = 185, energy frames `4 x ring + max(acoustic reach 30, D4 window + floor)` = 670 (715 with D4); the wake stream's own history keeps its configured sizes. When the history an estimate needs has been evicted (exact check: `first_token - 25` encoder frames and the acoustic or D4 reach before the first token's feature frame), the report carries `onset_unavailable` (`encoder_history_evicted` or `energy_history_evicted`) and no onset: never an estimate from a shorter window.

Lifetimes: destroying the wake stream drops the live confirm beam; a candidate change or expiry drops only the beam; a reset kills beams by epoch. `transcribe_continuous_full_command_result` is not supported with the switch.

Why detections can change: the confirm beam scores the wake stream's encoder frames (cached state from before the anchor, chunk phase from the wake origin) instead of a second encoder started from zero at the anchor; any token probability can cross the threshold either way.

Reports: `KwsOnsetReport.shared` (start state, frames requested and actual, ring frames and bytes, `onset_unavailable`) on live `confirm` reports; the replay `shared_front_end` key keeps live-confirm and fresh-confirm reports and latches apart and names the stage that latched, with a closing `shared_front_end_summary`.

Off path: additive form, no `KeywordStream` split. With the switch off no ring and no history are allocated, the decode path gains one `Option` check per chunk, and behaviour is intended to be byte-identical; the proof is a replay against the pre-C6 build (rightkit `ea382f068b6c683374dd3b64793def0ced48d3ae`, and the HeardRight-pinned 0.4.0 where relevant) AND the same new binary with the switch off versus on, comparing detections, timestamps, token frames, scores and fired frames; parsing an absent versus an explicit-off flag proves nothing about that. Not verified.
